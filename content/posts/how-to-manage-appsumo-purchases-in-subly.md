You track AppSumo deals by writing down four things the day you buy: what you paid, the purchase date, the refund deadline from the deal's terms, and who will use it. Then you test the deal on real work in its first days and decide keep or refund before the deadline, not after.

That's the whole system. The hard part isn't the list, it's remembering to look at it. A monthly subscription reminds you it exists every time it charges your card. A lifetime deal charges once and goes quiet, which is exactly why so many end up unused.

This guide gives you the routine, the columns worth tracking, and a way to tell whether anyone on the team actually uses each deal. It all works in a spreadsheet. If you'd rather not keep one, the last section shows how Subly does it from your AppSumo export.

## Why do lifetime deals pile up unused?

Lifetime deals are built to be easy yeses. The price is low, the offer ends soon, and paying once feels safer than another monthly bill. So you buy the tool you might need next quarter, then the one that could replace something you already pay for, then a second code to reach a higher tier.

None of these purchases is a mistake on its own. The trouble is what happens after. There's no renewal date, no invoice, no price rise email. Nothing ever asks "are you still using this?" A year later you have a page of products you've never opened and no memory of what each one cost.

The money adds up quietly. Twelve deals averaging $85 each is about $1,000, spent in small, forgettable pieces. That's real budget for a small business, and the refund window is the one moment you can get some of it back.

## How does the AppSumo refund window work?

Each AppSumo purchase comes with refund terms, and the window usually counts from the day you buy, not the day you first log in. That detail catches a lot of people: a deal you bought and never redeemed has been counting down the whole time.

Refund rules can differ between deals and can change over time, so don't rely on a number you remember from an older purchase. Before you buy, and again when you log the deal, read:

- **The deal page terms**, which say what's included and any conditions for that product.
- **AppSumo's refund policy**, linked from the site and your account, which sets the general rules.
- **Anything about stacked codes or upgrades**, because extra codes bought later may come with their own dates.

Then write the deadline down as a date. "Refund by March 14" is something you can act on. "A couple of months" isn't.

:::warning[The window runs whether you use the deal or not]
The countdown usually starts at purchase. If you buy during a busy week and plan to try the tool "when things calm down," check the deadline first. The safest habit is to redeem and set up a deal within a few days of buying it.
:::

## How do I decide whether to keep or refund a deal?

Give every deal a short, deliberate trial instead of waiting to see whether you drift into using it. This routine fits around real work:

:::steps
1. **Log it the day you buy.** Name, price, purchase date, refund deadline, and who it's for. Add two calendar reminders: one a few days after purchase, one about a week before the deadline.
2. **Redeem and set it up in the first few days.** Connect it to the accounts it needs and invite the person who will use it. Most deals that die, die here.
3. **Use it on one real job.** Not a demo project. Send the actual newsletter, record the actual client walkthrough, run the actual report. One real job tells you more than a week of clicking around.
4. **Write one sentence about how it went.** "Replaced our screen recordings, the team likes it" or "Missing the export we need." Future you will be glad you did.
5. **Decide at the second reminder.** Ask three questions: did we use it on real work, does it replace something we pay for, and does someone want to own it? Three yeses, keep. Any no, refund unless you have a clear reason.
:::

The third question matters more than it looks. A tool nobody owns is a tool nobody sets up properly, nobody notices when it breaks, and nobody defends at review time. If nobody raises a hand, that's your answer.

Be honest about "I might need it later," too. If a deal sat unused through its whole refund window, later rarely comes. Refund it and buy something when the need is real.

## What should I track for each lifetime deal?

A good record answers "what did we pay, what do we get, and who cares about it" at a glance. These columns cover it:

| Column | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Deal | Calmy, License Tier 2 |
| What you paid | $138 (two codes at $69) |
| Purchase date | Sep 12 |
| Refund deadline | The date from the deal's terms |
| Owner | Dana |
| Replaces | Our $19 a month scheduling tool |
| Status | Testing, kept or refunded |
| Notes | Missing a Zapier trigger, asked support |

The "Replaces" column is where lifetime deals earn their keep. The example above, $138 for two codes, pays for itself in about seven months if it really replaces the $19 a month tool. A deal that replaces nothing is money spent, however good the tool is.

Count stacked codes and upgrades in what you paid. And note any ongoing costs that come with a deal, such as add-ons, usage credits or a separate subscription for features outside the lifetime plan. Those are recurring charges, and they belong on the same list as your other subscriptions.

If you already keep a subscription spreadsheet, add your deals to it instead of starting a second one. The [subscription tracker spreadsheet guide](/blog/subscription-tracker-spreadsheet) has a template you can extend with a refund deadline column.

## How do I know if anyone actually uses a deal?

For a solo founder this is easy: you know. For a team it's the question that never gets asked, because the person who bought the deal is often not the person who would use it.

Three ways to find out without a meeting:

- **Ask the owner directly**, on the deal's record or in team chat: "Is anyone still using this?" A specific question about one tool gets an answer. A general "please review our tools" doesn't.
- **Check the tool's own admin page.** Many apps show the last login or the active users.
- **Look at what it was supposed to replace.** If you still pay for the old tool, the new one probably isn't doing the job.

Once the refund window has closed, the question changes. You can't get the money back, so ask whether the deal is still worth the setup and attention it takes. Review kept deals once a year alongside your subscriptions; the [30-minute software subscription audit](/blog/saas-subscription-audit) works for both. 1. On AppSumo, open **Account → Products** and export your purchases as a CSV.
