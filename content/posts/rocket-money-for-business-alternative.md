As of November 2026, there is no Rocket Money for business. Rocket Money is a personal budgeting and subscription app for US consumers, and its help center says it's only available to people in the United States with US-based banks. Its own pages show no business plan, and the "business" link on its homepage goes to Rocket Pro, a separate mortgage-lending product from Rocket Companies. Account sharing is designed for two people, typically you and a partner.

That doesn't make it a bad app. It makes it a household app. If you searched for "Rocket Money for business", you probably want the same clear list of everything that charges you, but for a company: several people, client money coming in, maybe a bank outside the US.

Below: what Rocket Money does well, what a business needs on top of that, and the tools you can use instead.

## What is Rocket Money good at?

For personal money, Rocket Money covers a lot, and the free plan is useful on its own. Plans and prices as of November 2026:

- **Free plan.** Your subscriptions in one view, spending categories, income vs. spend charts, a transaction log, your credit score, and notifications and balance alerts. By default it warns you when your checking account drops below $200.
- **Premium.** You choose what to pay, typically $7 to $14 a month according to a January 2026 Rocket Money article, which says the range is subject to change. It adds budgets, desktop access, manual transactions, real-time sync, transaction export and help cancelling subscriptions.
- **Premium+.** A fixed $15 a month plus tax. It adds Rowan, an AI personal-finance agent you talk to by text message, bill negotiation with no added success fee, access to apply for its high-yield savings account, and phone support.

Data comes in by linking your bank accounts through Plaid. If you live and bank in the US and want one app for the household budget, Rocket Money is a reasonable pick, and having bills negotiated for you is something none of the other options here do.

## What does a business need that a consumer app doesn't have?

A household has one or two people and money that mostly comes from a paycheck. A small business has more people, clients paying on their own schedules, and often more than one currency. These are the gaps that show up first.

- **Owners and seats.** A team needs more than two logins, and every tool needs one person who decides at renewal. Rocket Money's sharing is designed for two people, with no business seats or roles. Without owners, the Figma seat for the designer who left keeps renewing. [SaaS ownership for small teams](/blog/saas-ownership-for-small-teams) covers how to hand tools out.
- **Money coming in.** A $2,500 client retainer, Stripe subscriptions or a monthly payout matter as much as the bills. Rocket Money shows personal income vs. spend; business recurring revenue, such as Stripe, isn't mentioned on its pages. The guide to [tracking recurring revenue and client retainers](/blog/track-recurring-revenue) shows what to record.
- **More than one currency.** Webflow in dollars, a contractor in euros, a client paying in pounds. Rocket Money doesn't state any currency support.
- **Banks outside the US.** Rocket Money only works with US banks, so a Canadian or European business account can't be linked.
- **Alerts where the team works.** A renewal reminder on one person's phone doesn't reach the person who decides. If your team lives in Slack or Microsoft Teams, that's where a renewal gets read in time. Rocket Money sends push and email, and Rowan works by text message; no Slack or other business channel is mentioned.
- **A view for your accountant.** Something you can share once a month without handing over your login.
- **A separate business view.** Household groceries next to the company's Google Workspace bill make both harder to read.

None of this is a flaw in Rocket Money. It was built for a different job.

## What can a business use instead of Rocket Money?

It depends on how many people are involved, whether money comes in as well as goes out, and how much you are willing to type by hand. Prices are as of November 2026.

**Subly.** Subly tracks money going out and coming in: software, domains, insurance, client retainers, payouts and lifetime deals, in any currency. You get data in by uploading a bank or card statement (no bank login needed), forwarding receipts to your own Subly address, connecting a bank in the US, Canada or Europe through Plaid (read-only), or importing recurring revenue from Stripe on Business and Team. Every item can have an owner, and renewals go to the person who can decide. Alerts come by email on every plan, in Microsoft Teams, Discord, Telegram and push from Personal, and through the Slack app with Keep, Plan to cancel and Snooze buttons from Business. You can share a view-only copy of the month with your accountant, and Business and Team add a 13-week cash forecast with what-ifs. Business is $19 a month or $190 a year for 10 people and 5 banks. To be fair about the limits: Subly doesn't cancel subscriptions for you, negotiate prices or replace your accountant. Rocket Money's paid tiers offer cancellation help and bill negotiation.

**TrackMySubs.** Built for digital entrepreneurs, IT consultants and small businesses, it's simple manual tracking: type subscriptions in or import a CSV. It doesn't connect to banks, by choice. It sends email alerts before a payment, offers Slack or SMS alerts through Zapier, and supports over 160 currencies. The free plan covers up to 10 subscriptions, Unlimited is $10 a month or $99.99 a year, and multiple users are only on Enterprise, at $30 a month or $299.99 a year.

**CostLoop.** Aimed at freelancers, startups and small operations teams. It reads your Gmail or Outlook inbox through a Chrome extension with read-only access, and it detects recurring charges in a bank or card statement you upload as CSV; there is no live bank connection. Renewal alerts go by email. The free plan is capped at a few subscriptions (the pricing page says 5; check the current limit), Pro is $9 a month or $90 a year for one user, and Business at $39 a month or $390 a year adds a REST API, webhooks, workspaces and team visibility. It tracks costs only, and it's young: AppSumo lists the company as founded in May 2026.

**A spreadsheet.** Free, fully flexible and easy to share in Google Sheets, with formulas for monthly and yearly totals. The cost is upkeep: every charge is typed by hand, there are no renewal reminders without scripts or add-ons, and price rises go unnoticed until someone reads a statement. If you want to start there, the [subscription tracker spreadsheet](/blog/subscription-tracker-spreadsheet) has a free template.

## Can you use Rocket Money at home and Subly for the business?

Yes. You can keep Rocket Money for the household, where bill negotiation and the credit score are useful, and use Subly for the business, where owners, retainers and Slack alerts matter. Keeping them apart also keeps personal and company spending apart.

If you would rather have one tool, Subly also has a Personal plan for households at $5 a month or $48 a year as of November 2026, with 3 people and 1 connected bank. The [households page](/for/households) shows what it covers. It isn't a replacement for everything Rocket Money does, though: there is no bill negotiation or credit score.

## How do I choose?

Start from what the business actually needs:

- **You're in the US, it's personal money, and you want bills negotiated:** Rocket Money.
- **A handful of tools, one or two people, and you're happy typing:** TrackMySubs or a spreadsheet.
- **Most of your receipts sit in Gmail or Outlook and you only track costs:** CostLoop.
- **A team with owners, money coming in, several currencies, a bank outside the US or alerts in Slack or Teams:** Subly.

Whatever you pick, start the same way: get every recurring charge from the last 12 months onto one list and give each one an owner.
