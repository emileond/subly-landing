Probably not yet. SaaS management platforms such as Cledara, Zylo and Vertice are built for finance, IT and procurement teams that buy software at scale, and their prices reflect that. As of November 2026, Cledara publishes prices from £75 a month for up to 20 apps, £200 a month for up to 75, and from £500 a month above that, with paid add-ons on top. Zylo is enterprise and quote-only. Vertice, which announced its acquisition of Vendr in June 2026, lists "starting from" prices in the tens of thousands of dollars.

For a team under about 20 people, most of the value of SaaS management comes down to three answers: what you pay for, who owns each tool, and what renews when. A lighter tool covers that, and so does a well-kept list if someone keeps it up.

A platform is still the right call in specific cases, covered below, along with what each one costs and a checklist of what a small team actually needs.

## What is a SaaS management platform, and who is it for?

The platforms in this post go beyond tracking. They can pay for your tools, control who buys new ones, manage who has access, and help with contracts and renewals. Cledara names finance, procurement, IT and operations teams as its audience. Zylo describes itself as "The Enterprise Standard for SaaS and AI Spend Management." Vertice sells procurement products.

If nobody on your team has one of those roles, most of that machinery has no one to run it.

## What do Cledara, Zylo and Vertice cost?

### Cledara

Cledara is London-based and publishes its prices in GBP. As of November 2026:

- **Basic:** £75 a month or £750 a year, for up to 20 apps, with Xero and QuickBooks.
- **Premium:** £200 a month or £2,000 a year, for up to 75 apps. Adds NetSuite, automatic invoice capture, advanced reporting and forecasting, and 10 active Cledara Spend members.
- **Pro:** from £500 a month or from £5,000 a year. Adds Okta SSO and 50 Cledara Spend members. Extra members are £5 a month.
- **Add-ons:** Spend Optimisation, IT Management and Software Compliance at £150 a month each. Cledara Agent for invoice collection or user management at £200 a month each.

Every plan includes free virtual cards and unlimited API access. Cledara also offers approval workflows, spend limits and budgets, onboarding and offboarding, and renewal alerts. Duplicate-tool and price-increase alerts come with the Spend Optimisation add-on (£150 a month). Its pricing page shows no free plan or trial.

### Zylo

Zylo is built for large enterprises. Its customer logos include Atlassian, Intuit, Salesforce, Adobe, Netflix and The Home Depot, and Gartner named it a Leader in its Magic Quadrant for SaaS Management Platforms in 2024, 2025 and 2026. It publishes no prices. Every tier says "Request a Quote."

### Vertice (which now includes Vendr)

Vertice announced its acquisition of Vendr on June 1, 2026, and Vendr's pricing page now redirects to Vertice's. Vertice lists "starting from" prices per product: $20,000 for Cloud Cost Optimization, $30,000 for Intake-to-Procure and $40,000 for SaaS Purchasing. The page does not say whether those are yearly figures. The next step is booking a demo, and no free tier is shown.

## When is Cledara the right call?

Cledara does things a light tracker does not try to do. It makes sense when one or more of these is true:

- **You want a company virtual card per tool**, so each tool is paid on its own card.
- **You need approval workflows** before anyone buys new software.
- **You want onboarding and offboarding** handled in the same place as spend.
- **You use NetSuite** and want software spend connected to it.
- **You manage more than 75 apps**, which puts you on its Pro tier anyway.

Zylo and Vertice are a different conversation. If you have a procurement function and contracts in the tens of thousands, talk to them. If you are 8 people with a shared card, you are not who they built for.

## What does a small team actually need?

Under about 20 people, the problems are rarely about controlling purchases. They are about tools nobody remembers, renewals nobody sees coming, and seats for people who left. You fix those with information that reaches the right person in time:

:::checklist
- **One list** of every recurring charge, built from your bank and card statements rather than from memory
- **An owner per tool**: the person who can say keep or cancel
- **Renewal alerts to that owner**, in Slack or email, early enough to act
- **Price-rise alerts**, so a plan that went up does not renew unnoticed
- **A monthly review** of what changed: new tools, price rises, upcoming renewals
- **Budgets per team or client**, so spend shows up against what it is for
:::

The list is the foundation. Twelve months of statements catch the annual plans that monthly views miss; the [guide to finding recurring charges on a bank statement](/blog/find-recurring-charges-on-bank-statement) walks through it. For the ownership side (what an owner does, who approves new tools and how to stop paying for seats of people who left), see [who owns this tool?](/blog/saas-ownership-for-small-teams). If you want a one-off cleanup first, the [30-minute SaaS subscription audit](/blog/saas-subscription-audit) gets the list to a clean starting point.

## What other light options are there?

A spreadsheet is the free starting point. It is flexible, but every charge is entered by hand, it sends no reminders without scripts or add-ons, and it tends to drift once the person who built it leaves.

Two other light trackers aimed at small businesses, as of November 2026:

- **TrackMySubs** is built for digital entrepreneurs, IT consultants and small businesses. It has a free plan for up to 10 subscriptions, Unlimited at $10 a month or $99.99 a year, and Enterprise at $30 a month or $299.99 a year, which adds multiple users. Data comes in by hand or by CSV import, with no bank connection. Alerts go by email, and to Slack or SMS through Zapier.
- **CostLoop** is built for freelancers, startups and small operations teams; AppSumo lists it as founded in May 2026. Pro is $9 a month or $90 a year for one user. Business is $39 a month or $390 a year and adds a REST API, webhooks, workspaces and team visibility. It reads bank or card CSV files without a bank connection, and can scan Gmail and Outlook through a Chrome extension. Renewal alerts go by email.

## Where Subly fits, and what it does not do

:::subly[How Subly helps]
Upload a bank or card statement (PDF or CSV) and Subly finds the recurring charges, so your list starts from what you really pay. You can also forward receipts to your own Subly address, connect a bank through Plaid (read-only) or import a spreadsheet. Every item can have an owner, and items without one show up on **Today** until someone takes them. Renewals go to the owner by email on every plan, and on Business and Team the Slack app lets them press Keep, Plan to cancel or Snooze from the message. Subly flags price rises ("Notion Plus went up 20%: $10 → $12"), sends a monthly summary in plain words, and budgets per category or project speak up at 80% and again when crossed.
:::

What Subly does not do, so you can compare honestly:

- **No virtual cards.** It reads transactions; it cannot move money or pay anyone.
- **It does not cancel subscriptions or negotiate prices.** It tells the owner in time, and the owner cancels.
- **No SSO-based discovery.** It finds tools from statements, receipts and bank connections, not from your team's logins.
- **It does not replace your accounting software** or your accountant.

## How to decide

If one of the five reasons in the Cledara section is a real need today, a platform is worth its price. If none is, work through the checklist above. You can move up to a platform later, when you have the apps and the people to run it.
