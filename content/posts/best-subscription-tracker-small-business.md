The best subscription tracker for a small business is the one that matches how you work: how data gets in, who needs to see it, and whether you track money coming in as well as going out. If you only want to type a short list and get an email before renewals, a cheap manual tracker or a spreadsheet does the job. To start from a statement, route renewals to whoever decides, and see retainers next to costs, you need more than a list.

Below are the six things that matter, a comparison table, and a short section on each of eight options: who it's for, what's good, the limits, and the price as of November 2026.

## What matters in a subscription tracker for a small business?

These are the questions we asked of every product, and they work for any tool not on this list.

- **How data gets in.** Typing works for a dozen items. Past that, you want the tool to find what you forgot, from a statement, a bank connection, your inbox or a CSV. Bank connections are often limited by country; statement uploads work anywhere.
- **Alerts where the team works.** An email before a renewal is the minimum. If your team lives in Slack or Microsoft Teams, alerts there get read in time.
- **Owners and seats.** Someone has to decide keep or cancel before a tool renews, so check whether items can have owners and how many people a plan includes. See [who owns each tool](/blog/saas-ownership-for-small-teams).
- **Money coming in.** Agencies, studios and consultants get paid on retainers too. Most trackers only count costs, which leaves half of your recurring money out. See [how to track client retainers](/blog/track-recurring-revenue).
- **Currencies.** If you pay a server in euros and your design tools in dollars, you want each item kept in its own currency and totals in yours.
- **Price.** A tracker should cost a fraction of what it helps you cut. On free plans, the item limit matters most.

How we checked: we read each vendor's pricing and help pages in November 2026 (Wallos is open source, so we read its project page). Where a vendor's own pages disagree, we say so instead of picking a number.

## Subly

**Who it's for:** small businesses, agencies and freelancers that want to start from a bank or card statement and track money in and out in one place.

**What's good:** you upload a PDF or CSV statement, with no bank login, and Subly finds the recurring charges and points out the ones that look forgotten, renew soon or went up. You can also connect a bank (US, Canada and Europe, through Plaid, read-only), forward receipts, import recurring revenue from Stripe on Business and Team, import a spreadsheet, or type items in. Client retainers sit next to software and insurance, in any of 150+ currencies, with totals in yours.

Every item can have an owner, and renewals go to the person who can decide. Email alerts are on every plan; push, Discord, Telegram and Microsoft Teams from Personal; and the Slack app, with Keep / Plan to cancel / Snooze buttons, from Business. Business and Team add a 13-week cash forecast with what-ifs.

**The limits:** Subly is read-only. It can't move money or pay anyone, so there are no company cards or payment approvals. It doesn't cancel subscriptions for you, negotiate prices or replace your accounting software. The Free plan reads one statement and tracks up to 20 items, and the Slack app and the forecast need Business or Team.

**Price as of November 2026:** Free $0. Personal $5/month or $48/year (3 people, 1 bank). Business $19/month or $190/year (10 people, 5 banks). Team $49/month or $490/year (20 people, 10 banks). Every new account starts with a 14-day Business trial, no credit card, and keeps Free afterward.

## TrackMySubs

**Who it's for:** digital entrepreneurs, IT consultants and small businesses, including agencies and IT providers that manage several clients.

**What's good:** it's a focused manual tracker. Set up your list with one CSV upload, organize it with folders and tags, and see a calendar of what's due when. Email alerts arrive before each payment and can go to several addresses. It supports 160+ currencies, crypto included, converted to yours at a daily rate. It deliberately doesn't connect to banks, which some prefer.

**The limits:** there's no bank or inbox import, so finding new subscriptions is up to you. Slack and SMS alerts only work through Zapier. Multiple users are only on the top tier, and it's positioned as expense management, with no income tracking stated.

**Price as of November 2026:** Free for up to 10 subscriptions. Unlimited $10/month or $99.99/year. Enterprise, which adds multiple users, $30/month or $299.99/year. No trial of the paid plans is stated.

If you work alone, want simple manual tracking with email alerts, and don't need income or bank import, TrackMySubs is a sensible, inexpensive choice.

## CostLoop

**Who it's for:** freelancers, startups and small operations teams. It's a young product; its AppSumo listing gives a founding date in May 2026.

**What's good:** you can upload a bank or card CSV and it detects recurring charges, without a bank connection. A Chrome extension scans Gmail and Outlook with read-only access for subscriptions you didn't know about. Pro adds unused-seat and duplicate-tool detection, and Business adds a REST API, webhooks and workspaces, with no per-seat charges.

**The limits:** there's no live bank feed, inbox scanning needs the Chrome extension, and the API is only on the $39 tier. It tracks costs only, and no Slack alerts are stated. Its own pages disagree on the free-plan limit and on whether totals convert between currencies, so check both before you rely on them.

**Price as of November 2026:** a free plan capped at a handful of subscriptions (the pricing page says 5; check the current limit). Pro $9/month or $90/year for one user. Business $39/month or $390/year.

If most of your subscriptions show up as receipts in Gmail or Outlook and you only track costs, CostLoop's inbox scanning is a good reason to pick it.

## SubTracker

**Who it's for:** households and families. Its Family tier is labeled "For families & small teams," but the product is built around a household's subscriptions.

**What's good:** the pricing page lists no subscription limit on the free plan, and a quick-add library of 180+ services makes manual entry quick. Calendar sync with Google, Apple and Outlook puts renewals where you already look. Plus adds renewal and price-hike alerts, and Family adds up to 10 members with role-based access. It never links to your bank and handles USD, EUR, GBP, CHF and 20+ more currencies.

**The limits:** it's manual only, with no bank or inbox import. Alerts need a paid plan, no Slack is mentioned, and no income tracking is stated.

**Price as of November 2026:** Free. Plus $5.90/month or $49/year. Family $9.90/month or $79/year. Lifetime $129 one time. New accounts get 14 days of Plus, no card required.

If you want cheap, simple manual tracking for a household or a very small team, SubTracker does it well.

## Rocket Money

**Who it's for:** US consumers. Rocket Money's pages show no business plan, and it only works for people in the US with US banks.

**What's good:** the free plan shows your subscriptions, spending categories and income against spending, from bank accounts linked through Plaid. Premium+ adds Rowan, an AI agent that works over text message, and bill negotiation, which as of November 2, 2026 comes with no success fee on that plan.

**The limits:** account sharing is designed for two people, a primary holder and a partner, with no business seats or roles. Alerts come by push and email; no Slack is mentioned.

**Price as of November 2026:** Free. Premium is pay what you think is fair; a January 2026 article from Rocket Money put the typical range at $7 to $14 a month and says it's subject to change. Premium+ is $15/month plus tax.

If you live and bank in the US, Rocket Money is a good pick for your personal subscriptions, especially if you want bills negotiated for you. Keep your business on a separate tool.

## Wallos

**Who it's for:** people who want to run their own tracker on their own server, usually individuals and households, or a small team with someone who already self-hosts.

**What's good:** it's open source under GPL-3.0, and your data stays on your server. Alerts go out by email, Discord, Pushover, Telegram, Gotify and webhooks. It supports household members and standard users with OIDC sign-in, multiple currencies (rates come from the Fixer API, which needs a free key), an API, and AI recommendations through ChatGPT, Gemini or a local Ollama model.

**The limits:** you run the server, updates and backups, with no vendor support. Data goes in by hand; no bank or inbox import is stated.

**Price as of November 2026:** free to use; hosting is your own cost.

If you self-host already, Wallos is the natural choice.

## Cledara

**Who it's for:** finance, procurement, IT and operations teams managing software spend. It's what to look at when you've outgrown small-business tools.

**What's good:** it issues free virtual cards, so every tool is paid on a card you control. It adds approval workflows, spend limits and budgets, onboarding and offboarding, Xero and QuickBooks integrations, and renewal alerts, with duplicate-tool and price-increase alerts in the Spend Optimisation add-on. An add-on, Cledara Agent, signs into your tools to collect invoices.

**The limits:** no free plan or trial is stated, and sign-up asks for company verification documents. The entry plan covers up to 20 apps, prices are in pounds, and no income tracking is stated.

**Price as of November 2026:** Basic £75/month or £750/year (up to 20 apps). Premium £200/month or £2,000/year (up to 75 apps). Pro from £500/month or from £5,000/year. Add-ons start at £150/month each.

If you need virtual cards and approvals across a larger company, Cledara is built for that.

## A spreadsheet

**Who it's for:** a business with under about 15 items, mostly in one currency, and someone who likes a monthly check.

**What's good:** it's free or already paid for, fully flexible, and shares no data with a vendor. Formulas give you monthly and yearly totals. Our [subscription tracker spreadsheet](/blog/subscription-tracker-spreadsheet) has a free template with the columns and formulas.

**The limits:** every charge is entered by hand, there are no renewal reminders without scripts or add-ons, and price rises go unnoticed until someone compares a statement. Currencies are converted by hand, copies drift, and the sheet tends to be forgotten once the person who built it leaves.

**Price:** free.

Zylo and Vertice (which announced its acquisition of Vendr in June 2026) are enterprise tools sold through a sales team, with quotes or demos instead of self-serve plans.

## How do I choose a subscription tracker?

Find the line that sounds like you:

- **Under about 15 items in one currency:** a spreadsheet, reviewed once a month.
- **Simple manual tracking, cheaply:** TrackMySubs if you work alone or run a small agency; SubTracker if it's mostly a household.
- **Costs only, with inbox scanning:** CostLoop, once you've checked its current free-plan limit and currency handling.
- **You want to self-host:** Wallos.
- **Personal subscriptions in the US:** Rocket Money, kept separate from the business.
- **Start from a statement, track money in and out, owners and Slack alerts:** Subly.
- **Virtual cards and approvals at a larger company:** Cledara.
- **An enterprise with a procurement team:** Zylo or Vertice.

Whichever you choose, run one [subscription audit](/blog/saas-subscription-audit) when you set it up, so the tracker starts with a complete list.
